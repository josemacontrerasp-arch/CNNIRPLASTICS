OVERVIEW
--------
Two CNN architectures were trained and evaluated on the FTIR plastics dataset
(6000 spectra, 6 classes: HDPE, LDPE, PP, PS, PVC, PET).

  1D CNN: operates directly on normalized absorbance spectra (1868 points)
  2D CNN: operates on Gramian Angular Field (GAF) images (64x64, 2 channels:
          GASF + GADF), derived by downsampling spectra to 64 points before
          computing the GAF matrices.

Both use identical architecture depth:
  Conv block 1: Conv(64) -> Conv(64) -> MaxPool
  Conv block 2: Conv(64) -> Conv(64) -> MaxPool
  Head: Flatten -> Dense(64)+Dropout(0.2) x3 -> Dense(6, softmax)
  Optimizer: Adam (lr=1e-4), EarlyStopping (patience=100)
  Training: 5-fold stratified cross-validation, 70/30 train/val split per fold

================================================================================
SECTION 3 - SUMMARY & CONCLUSIONS
================================================================================

  Metric                    1D CNN       2D CNN       Delta
  ------                    ------       ------       -----
  Mean CV accuracy          99.62%       99.83%       +0.21 pp
  Best fold accuracy        99.83%      100.00%       +0.17 pp
  Mean training time/fold    270.8 s      585.8 s      2.16x slower
  Mean inference time          5.29 ms      5.65 ms    +0.36 ms (+6.8%)
    of which GAF transform       -           0.23 ms
    of which model fwd         5.29 ms      5.41 ms    +0.12 ms
  Robustness (24 samples)  24/24 (100%) 24/24 (100%)  identical

  Key observations:
  1. The 2D CNN (GAF-based) consistently outperforms the 1D CNN across all folds.
  2. The GAF transform adds only ~0.23 ms at inference — negligible overhead.
  3. At inference, both architectures are effectively equivalent in speed (~5.3 ms
     vs ~5.6 ms for a 5-model ensemble).
  4. The primary cost of the 2D approach is training time (2.16x longer), not
     inference time.
  5. Both models are fully robust to Gaussian noise (std=0.01) and vertical shift
     (0.05) across all 6 plastic classes.

  Recommendation: the 2D CNN is the better model for production — it is more
  accurate, equally fast at inference, and completely robust to the tested
  corruptions. The extra training time is a one-off cost.
